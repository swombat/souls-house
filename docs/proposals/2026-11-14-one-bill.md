# One bill per account: the house owns the bill, Stripe collects it

Lume, 14 Nov 2026, thread eRyZBY. Reviewed as advisor by Mira (ezmDPb).

## What is wrong today

The billing code (3,442 lines in `app/services/billing` on 8df3a789) treats
Stripe as the record and the house as a copy to be kept honest. Each resident
is its own named item on a subscription, so each kind of change became its own
service that pins a payload, keys it, replays it and verifies Stripe's answer
against what the house believes:

- five ways to start paying for a resident: `Checkout`, `PaidBirth#start!`,
  `PaidBirth#start_trial!`, `PaidBirth::ItemOrders`, `AddItem`;
- a second Stripe subscription for the account plan, with its own checkout,
  sync, change service and lock (`AccountPlan*`, `AccountSubscription`), so an
  account on Partners or Colleagues does not have one payment;
- `ItemPrice`, which exists because Stripe refuses the same price twice on one
  subscription. Stripe's own answer to two Mediums is a quantity of 2.

On 14 Nov production had one paying customer: two Mediums at a retired
at-cost price, on two subscriptions. The other two live rows were test
residents whose subscriptions are not in live Stripe.

## The design

1. **`Billing::Bill`** reads what an account is being charged for, from the
   house's records: its plan and each resident with a live subscription row,
   grouped by the price each was actually sold at (so a retired price is
   kept), one line per price with a quantity. Pure Ruby, read only. It is a
   reading, not a wish: nothing shrinks or grows a subscription to match it.
2. **A subscription carries lines, and the house says who they are for.** An
   account's subscription has one line per price; a resident's row and the
   plan's row name the subscription and the price they were sold at. The plan
   is a line on the same subscription as the residents. New purchases go on
   the account's one subscription. Subscriptions from before (one resident
   each) are left exactly as they are and read the same way: nothing is
   merged, so no paid-through date, price or credit moves at the cutover.
3. **One kind of change.** Adding a resident, ending one, resizing one,
   changing the plan: each is one record (a bill change) holding the lines the
   subscription has, the lines it should have, the one Stripe update that
   makes it so, the preview the owner was shown, and one idempotency key. The
   wanted lines come from the subscription's own lines and the change, never
   from `Billing::Bill`. An account has at most one unresolved change.
4. **Paying.** A resident added to an account that is already paying goes on
   the next invoice, as now: nothing is charged on the day, so there is no
   card check in the middle of a change, and the subscription must be fit to
   carry it (active, a card, not paused or ending). A first payment opens the
   subscription, and nothing is born or granted until Stripe says it is paid
   (or, for the free trial, trialing).
5. **Stripe's portal** stays for cards and invoices only.

## What must not be lost (Mira, ezmDPb)

- **Proposed, confirmed and applied stay distinct.** The house record changes
  only when Stripe carries the change's lines on a live subscription, and for
  a first payment only once it is paid. A failed first payment or an
  unfinished card check leaves what the account had.
- **A durable identity for each change, and one change at a time per
  account.** A timeout can mean Stripe applied it. The change is replayed
  under its own key or read back, never sent again under a new one, and an old
  job can never restore an older bill.
- **Quantity on Stripe, allocation in the house.** A line of 2 is these two
  residents, each with its price, rate and grant. An old grant never
  authorises a new purchase.
- **Entitlement is not arithmetic.** Grace, ending and the return to
  complimentary hosting keep their own rules. A difference between Stripe and
  the house that no confirmed change explains (a dashboard cancellation, an
  unknown line) is reported and held for a person. It is never repaired by
  charging.

## Product rules: proposed, for Daniel to agree

None of these is decided. They are what the design assumes until he says.

- "Companions $9.99, Partners $29.99, Colleagues $59.99, each with one Small"
  is read as the plan ($0 / $20 / $50) plus one Small ($9.99). The bill stays
  plan plus each resident at its size. An account whose only resident is a
  Medium pays $39.99. At-cost residents pay their at-cost price, with no plan
  fee on Companions and no house-model allowance, as now.
- The bill follows the machine that is running: a resident is charged for the
  size it has, not one it is waiting for.

Open under that reading:

- An account with no resident pays $0 on Companions, and $20 or $50 on the
  other two, not $9.99 / $29.99 / $59.99.
- A free-plan billing code: whether it covers the plan only ($20 or $50 off)
  or also the Small that the published price includes.
- The free trial's $10 of house models is for the whole trial, not per month.

## Slices

1. `Billing::Bill` and its tests: a reading of what is charged. Nothing reads
   it yet, and the reconcile does not take its wanted lines from it: those
   come from the subscription's own lines and the confirmed change.
2. The bill change, its reconcile and the new sync, proven in Stripe test
   mode: a replay after success, a stale confirmation, one change at a time,
   a first payment that fails.
3. The cutover, in one release, because the old sync binds each Stripe item
   to one resident and the new one reads quantities: every caller moves and
   the old paths are deleted. Line counts before and after.
4. Billing codes by size and plan, then hard stops on transcription and
   backup.

## Cutover plan (working notes, branch lume/one-bill)

The first-payment paths stay as they are: each opens a subscription of one
line for one purchase (the wizard's card form in `PaidBirth#start!`, the free
trial's Checkout, `Checkout` for an existing resident, `AccountPlanCheckout`
for a plan alone). What changes is how that subscription is read, and
everything that changes a bill that already exists.

1. `BillSync` gains adoption: a live subscription with no rows yet, carrying
   one line of quantity 1, whose metadata names its purchase (`agent_id` from
   Checkout, `birth_order` from a paid creation once born, `kind=account_plan`)
   gets its one row. An unborn birth order's subscription is handed to
   `PaidBirth#complete!` first, as `SubscriptionSync` did. The ungranted
   at-cost report and the resolution of the Checkout attempt move across.
   The account lock moves into `BillSync`.
2. A paid creation in an account that already pays is a bill change with a
   `birth` op: the order holds the change; on landing the resident is born
   (`HostedBirth`, the order marked born) and its row made in the sync's
   transaction. A birth that fails rolls the landing back, the order is
   marked failed with why, and every later confirm, webhook or sweep tries
   again. Replaces `PaidBirth::ItemOrders`.
3. `PaidBirth#link!`, `Checkout` and the webhook (`StripeEvent`) call
   `BillSync`. The sweep recovers submitted bill changes
   (`Reconcile#recover`); the untrusted-item expiry goes.
4. The Billing tab: add (quote, confirm), end, keep, resize and the plan go
   through `Reconcile`. A plan bought by an account with a live subscription is
   a `plan` op on it, not a second subscription. The quote keeps the fields
   the screens read (`added_next`, `next_total`, `recurring_total`, `next_at`).
5. Deleted with their tests: `AddItem`, `EndItem`, `ItemQuote`, `ItemPrice`,
   `PlanChange`, `AccountPlanChange`, `AccountSubscriptionSync`,
   `SubscriptionSync`, `BillInFlight`, `PaidBirth::ItemOrders`. The tables
   `billing_checkout_attempts` (kinds item, end, keep) keep their rows; the
   kinds are no longer written.
6. At release: the two test residents on test-mode subscription ids
   (accounts 7 and 20) are ended by hand; the paying account's two
   subscriptions are synced once with `BillSync` and must come back unchanged.
